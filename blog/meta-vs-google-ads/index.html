Meta Ads vs Google Ads: which one should you use?

They're not competitors. One creates demand, the other captures it. Here's how to decide where your next dollar goes.

By Mohamed Elmlegey · Published 2026-10-14 · 6 min read

Google Ads captures people who are already searching for what you sell, so it usually converts faster. Meta Ads creates demand by showing your product to people who weren't searching yet, so it scales further. Most growing businesses need both: Google for existing demand, Meta for new demand.

The core difference

Google AdsMeta Ads
How people find youThey search for itYou interrupt their scroll
Best atCapturing existing demandCreating new demand
Main leverKeywords, feed and bidsCreative and offer
LimitSearch volume caps your scaleCreative fatigue

When Google Ads should come first

  • People already search for your product or service by name or category.
  • You sell urgent or high-intent services (repairs, clinics, legal, B2B software).
  • You have a product catalog that fits Google Shopping.

When Meta Ads should come first

  • Your product is new, visual or impulse-friendly.
  • Few people search for it yet.
  • You can produce creatives regularly.

How to split the budget

Start by covering the search demand that already exists for your brand and category on Google. Put the rest into Meta (and TikTok or Snapchat in the Gulf) to create new demand, then let results decide. Judge both channels by cost per sale or qualified lead, not by clicks, and remember that Meta often drives searches that Google later converts.

By industry

  • E-commerce: Meta for prospecting and retargeting, Google Shopping for buyers ready to compare. See e-commerce.
  • Clinics: Meta and TikTok messaging for treatments, Google Search for urgent needs. See clinics.
  • Real estate: Meta for volume with qualifying forms, Google for people searching specific projects. See real estate.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Ads more expensive than Meta Ads?

Cost per click is usually higher on Google, but intent is also higher. Compare cost per sale or qualified lead, not cost per click.

Can I run only one platform?

Yes, especially with a small budget. Start where your buyers already are, then add the second platform once the first is profitable.
